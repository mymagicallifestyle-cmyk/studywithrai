✦ Chapter 2: Time Management and Timetables ✦

Yes, time management is the first thing you should work on! Without knowing how you spend your time, it becomes difficult to stay consistent with your studies or other activities. A fixed routine can help you manage your day more effectively.

But remember, don't create an unrealistic, overly perfect timetable that you won't be able to follow after two days!

For now, don't make a timetable just yet. Instead, observe the tasks you usually do on a school day. Once you've identified them, write them down.

Avoid assigning exact timestamps to every activity (except your waking-up and study times). This prevents your timetable from becoming too strict and difficult to follow.

Instead, divide your day into parts — morning, afternoon, evening, and night — and list your activities in the order you usually do them. Be honest with yourself, and include as many details as possible. I followed this method too, and it really helped me understand how I was spending my time!

Let's take a look at what a typical routine might look like:

✨ Part of the Day 📌 Task
Morning (7 am)Wake up
MorningScroll for a little while
MorningPack your school bag (or do it the night before)
MorningHave breakfast
MorningGet dressed
MorningLeave for school
AfternoonReturn from school
AfternoonHave lunch
AfternoonScroll through your phone again
EveningTuition or study session 1
EveningAnother tuition or study session
NightReturn from tuition and take a break
Night (on tuition days)Self-study or homework
Night (optional)Hobbies or more screen time

Now that you have a clear picture of your current routine, you can identify which habits need to change and which activities you would like to include.

It's not just about changing your habits; it's also about changing when you do them. For example, you don't necessarily have to scroll through your phone immediately after waking up. You could check it later, perhaps before leaving for school. Keep it brief — check notifications, reply to messages, or take care of anything important.

You can also make more room for your hobbies and build peaceful morning and night routines. This helps you enjoy your free time without constantly feeling guilty about unfinished tasks.

(Using your phone immediately after waking up can also affect your morning routine. I'll discuss this in a future chapter!)

After making a few adjustments, your updated routine might look something like this:

✨ Part of the Day 📌 Task
Morning (7 am)Wake up, stretch, and drink some water
MorningPack your school bag (preferably the night before)
MorningHave breakfast
MorningGet dressed and check your phone briefly
MorningLeave for school
AfternoonReturn from school
AfternoonHave lunch
AfternoonEnjoy some screen time
AfternoonSpend time on hobbies, go outside, and touch some grass — no, seriously! :)
EveningTuition or study session 1
EveningAnother tuition or study session
NightReturn from tuition and take a break
NightRelax, journal, and follow your night routine

And there you have it! You now have a basic idea of how to create a timetable that suits your lifestyle and daily responsibilities.

Notice how it includes time for hobbies, relaxation, screen time, journalling, and even going outside. A productive routine doesn't have to mean studying every single hour of the day!

In fact, when you complete your tasks and studies on time instead of constantly putting them off, you create more opportunities to enjoy your free time.

On the other hand, spending the entire day scrolling while telling yourself that you'll study later can leave you rushing through everything at the last minute. This often results in unnecessary stress and less effective studying.

Remember, a timetable isn't meant to control every minute of your life. It's simply a tool that helps you use your time wisely.

Good things take time. And time can be managed by a timetable.
