✦ Chapter 2: Time management and time tables

Yes, time management is the first thing you should do! Because if you don’t know what time you will study or scroll or do anything else, you cannot be consistent with anything. Make a fixed schedule. And no, don’t make a very unrealistic, and too perfect timetable which you won't be able to follow after 2 days. First, for now, don’t make a timetable, just observe the tasks you usually do on your school days. After observing, write them. Don’t give time stamps (except waking up times and study times) because those are the traps; these make your timetable too perfect. Write the part of the day when you are doing the tasks, this helps you to overlook through your tasks. I also did it the same way, trust me! Just write the things you do order by order in a list. Be honest with yourself, nothing to lie about. Add as many detailed tasks as you can.

Let's assume your present routine looks like this:

✨Part of the day 📌Task
Morning (7 am) Wake up
Morning Scroll a little
Morning Pack bag (maybe the night before)
Morning Breakfast
Morning Get dressed
Morning Leave for school
Afternoon Come back from school
Afternoon Eat lunch
Afternoon Scroll again
Evening Tuition/study session 1
Evening More tuition/ study session 2
Night Come back from tuition & chill/ break after study sessions
Night (on tuition days) Self study/ homeworks
Night (optional) Hobbies/more scrolling
Now you can identify which parts need to change and which habits to add. Not only change the habits, but also their placement. Like you don’t have to scroll right after waking up. You can check your phone later, like before going to school, maybe. No scrolling, just a little notification check, messaging back people, or even sending something important. You can give hobbies more time, create a peaceful morning and night routine as well. This saves you from guilt, as well as managing your morning well! (Watching phone in the morning right after waking up is also harmful, I'll be explaining it to you in the following chapters) Now your updated routine might look like this: Part of the day Task Morning (7 am) Wake up Morning Fresh up + drink water + stretch Morning Pack bag (maybe the night before) Morning Breakfast Morning Get dressed + check phone Morning Leave for school Afternoon Come back from school Afternoon Eat lunch Afternoon Scroll+enjoy! Afternoon Do hobblies, go out and touch grass no like fr :) Evening Tuition/study session 1 Evening More tuition/ study session 2 Night Come back from tuition & chill/ break after study sessions Night Chill + journal+ night routine Now you know how to build timetables suitable for your needs. Nothing complex, nothing too boring, right? It includes hobbies, chill time, scrolling, journalling, and going out! What can be more fun than that? And if you stick to your routine, and complete your tasks and studying on time instead of procrastinating, this gives you more time to enjoy your life! And if you scroll the whole day, saying you have a lot of studying, and you'll eventually do it later, well, you are going to end up cramming at the last minute with very low-quality results. The more time, the more days you are gonna spend on that task or studying, it will make it more perfect. Good things take time. And time can be managed by a timetable. Timetables are useful only when you actually follow them, and you only follow them when they relate to your own life, not others. Stop copying those Pinterest-perfect routines, and make your own. Yes, you can take ideas and habits from them, but don’t forget to adjust them to your own way. For how many days have you procrastinated for studying for a test and chosen comfort instead? And then, realising only the night before the test? Yes, I know I've been there, and I know how to fix it. You just have to finish the big chunk and do the remaining secondary tasks later. This is a simple habit that most of us fail to adapt. This comes from a timetable that is well planned and realistic. Because your time is managed properly, and giving time to primary or high-priority tasks. How to actually stick to timetables? To actually stick to your timetable, you need to avoid the following mistakes: • Making a perfect timetable: As I explained earlier, it looks good on paper, but actually very hard to follow in reality. And when you miss one task, you feel like the whole day is crashing and eventually cannot even do the other tasks. Solution- Adjust it according to your needs. If one primary task is missed, do it, leaving the secondary task. • Depending on motivation: you follow the timetable when you “feel like it”. The fact that you will never actually feel like it. Solution - Build discipline. Just do it now, today. (I will talk about building discipline in the following chapter.) • “I’ll start from tomorrow” mindset: tomorrow never comes. How many times have you said that you are gonna start tomorrow, and the next day you either forget about it, or when you start doing it, you feel bored.
Solution-Just start it today. Don’t think about it too much, just do it. By the time you get the reward, you will be motivated to do it again the next day, and eventually it creates a cycle, and it becomes discipline or a habit.
